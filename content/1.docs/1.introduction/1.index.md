The E-field Parallel Imaging Correlator (EPIC) is a direct-imaging correlator architecture for radio telescopes composed of many antennas. The antenna voltages are not cross-correlated in pairs to form visibilities. Instead, EPIC proceeds as follows (Morales 2011; Thyagarajan et al. 2017):

1. The channelized antenna electric fields are gridded onto a regular aperture-plane grid.
2. A spatial Fourier transform of the grid yields an instantaneous electric-field image of the sky.
3. The intensity and polarization products are formed by pixel-wise multiplication of these images, followed by temporal averaging.

The resulting images are mathematically equivalent to those obtained by gridding and Fourier transforming visibilities. The computational cost, however, scales with the number of grid cells, $\mathcal{O}(N_g \log_2 N_g)$, rather than with the number of antenna pairs, $\mathcal{O}(N_a^2)$.

EPIC operates as a commensal back end at the Long Wavelength Array station at the Sevilleta National Wildlife Refuge (LWA-SV), New Mexico. The implementation described in this documentation is the `epic++` imager, from the `phoenix` branch of [LWA_EPIC](https://github.com/epic-astronomy/LWA_EPIC/tree/phoenix), version 0.15. It forms an all-sky image of the visible hemisphere for every 40 µs spectrum produced by the station F-engine. That is 25 000 images per second per 25 kHz channel per polarization, which are integrated to a cadence of 80 ms.

## Motivation

A radio telescope composed of many antennas samples the spatial coherence of the incident radiation field through the visibility, $V_{ab} = \langle E_a E_b^{*} \rangle_t$, between antennas $a$ and $b$. A conventional FX correlator works in two stages:

- **F stage.** Each antenna signal is channelized.
- **X stage.** The product $E_a E_b^*$ is formed for all $N_a(N_a-1)/2$ antenna pairs and averaged in time.

Both the arithmetic cost and the output data rate of the X stage are therefore proportional to $N_a^2$. For the radio telescopes proposed for low-frequency cosmology and transient science, which combine large numbers of densely packed antennas (e.g. HERA, CHIME, HIRAX, SKA-Low), this scaling dominates the cost and power budget of the correlator.

The output rate of an FX correlator is also inversely proportional to the integration time. Visibility data rates are therefore typically controlled by averaging over intervals of seconds, which suppresses sensitivity to transients on millisecond timescales.

## Principle of direct imaging

EPIC rests on the observation that the gridding kernel applied to visibilities in optimal map-making is the cross-correlation of the two antennas' aperture illumination patterns. The sum over gridded visibilities can therefore be rewritten as the correlation of two gridded electric-field distributions. Applying the convolution theorem gives the imaging equation:

$$
I(\hat{\mathbf s}) = \Big\langle \Big|\, \mathcal{F}\Big[\, \textstyle\sum_a \mathcal{W}_a(\mathbf u - \mathbf u_a)\, E_a \Big](\hat{\mathbf s}) \Big|^2 \Big\rangle_t ,
$$

Here $\mathcal{W}_a$ is the aperture illumination of antenna $a$, and $\mathbf u_a$ is its position in wavelengths.

The formulation places no constraint on antenna placement, and it admits non-identical antennas through antenna-specific gridding kernels. This distinguishes EPIC from the earlier FFT telescope (Tegmark & Zaldarriaga 2009, 2010), which requires identical elements on regular or hierarchical grids. The derivation, cost model and its limitations are given in [The EPIC Algorithm](/docs/theory/epic-algorithm).

## The LWA-SV implementation

The principal parameters of the LWA-SV imager are summarized below.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Antennas | 256 dual-polarization dipole stands, ~110 m × 100 m |
| Frequency range | 10–88 MHz (station); 25 kHz channels |
| Field of view | Visible hemisphere; science extraction above 10° elevation |
| Image size | 128 × 128 pixels, ≈1.06° at zenith |
| Bandwidth per GPU | 128 channels (3.2 MHz); 19.2 MHz over six nodes |
| Polarization products | $XX^*$, $YY^*$, $XY^*$ (with $YX^* = (XY^*)^*$) |
| Imaging rate | 25 000 images s⁻¹ per channel per polarization |
| Output cadence | 80 ms; 1.28 s accumulations archived as FITS |

The imager produces four data products:

- **Pixel spectrograms.** Spectrograms of a $5\times5$-pixel region around each source in a dynamically maintained watch list, stored in a PostgreSQL database and accessible through the [Waterfalls](/waterfalls) application.
- **All-sky images.** Accumulated all-sky image cubes in FITS format.
- **Daily digest.** Stokes I and V spectra of each watched source.
- **Live video.** A real-time Stokes I video stream of the sky, [EPIC TV](https://livetv.epic-astronomy.org).
