## Calibration of direct imagers

The signal chain of each antenna applies a complex, frequency-dependent gain to the incident voltage:

$$
E_a = g_a\, E_a^{\rm true} + n_a .
$$

In an FX correlator the visibilities take the form $V_{ab} = g_a g_b^* V_{ab}^{\rm true}$. Because the visibilities are recorded, the gains may be determined and removed after correlation.

In a direct imager, the antenna signals are combined on the grid before any time averaging, and visibilities are not formed. The gain estimates must therefore be applied to the voltages, $E'_a = E_a / \hat g_a$, before gridding, and updated in real time. Malfunctioning antennas cannot be excised after imaging and must be flagged beforehand ([Memo 002](https://github.com/epic-astronomy/Memos/blob/master/PDFs/002_Searching_for_CGP.pdf)).

## EPICal

Beardsley et al. (2017) developed EPICal, following a proposal by Morales (2011). EPICal correlates the voltage of each antenna with the image pixel at the position $\hat{\mathbf s}_0$ of a calibrator source:

$$
K_{a,\hat{\mathbf s}_0} = \big\langle E_a(t)\, \tilde E'^{*}(\hat{\mathbf s}_0, t) \big\rangle_t .
$$

The image pixel is a weighted sum over all antennas, so $K_{a,\hat{\mathbf s}_0}$ contains the sum $\sum_b V_{ab}$, weighted by the current gain estimates. The self term is subtracted to give $C_{a,\hat{\mathbf s}_0}$. The updated gain is then the ratio of $C_{a,\hat{\mathbf s}_0}$ to its prediction from model visibilities $V^M_{ab}$:

$$
g_a^{(n+1)} = \frac{N_a\, C_{a,\hat{\mathbf s}_0}}{\sum_{b\ne a} W_b^{*}(\hat{\mathbf s}_0)\, e^{-2\pi i f\, \hat{\mathbf s}_0\cdot \mathbf r_b / c}\, V^{M}_{ab}} .
$$

The iteration is damped to suppress oscillation:

$$
g_a^{(n+1)} \leftarrow (1-\gamma)\, g_a^{(n+1)} + \gamma\, g_a^{(n)}, \qquad 0 \le \gamma < 1 .
$$

The damping corresponds to an effective integration time $t_{\rm eff} = t_{\rm cal}(1+\gamma)/(1-\gamma)$.

The algorithm has the following properties (Beardsley et al. 2017):

- **Cost.** The cost per time sample is $\mathcal{O}(N_a)$ per calibrator pixel, so it scales in the same way as EPIC itself.
- **Accuracy with a full sky model.** The gain noise exceeds that of visibility-based calibration by approximately 23%.
- **Accuracy with a single-source model.** The noise floor is lower than that of visibility-based calibration, because the correlation effectively beamforms on the calibrator.
- **Convergence.** In simulation, the gains converged within approximately 12 iterations (0.12 s).
- **LWA data.** On 2 s of LWA data, calibrating on Cygnus A and Cassiopeia A, image dynamic range increased from 80.4 to 133.1.
- **Extension.** Correlation against multiple pixels permits estimation of direction-dependent gains and of the antenna beam.

## Corrections applied at LWA-SV

EPICal is not implemented in the real-time LWA-SV imager (Kent et al. 2019; Krishnan et al. 2023), and `epic++` does not perform antenna-based gain calibration.

The imager applies a per-stand, per-polarization, per-channel complex weight. The weight is derived from the station model in the LWA Software Library (LSL) and recomputed whenever the station is retuned:

$$
\phi_a(f) = \frac{\exp\!\big[\,2\pi i f\,\big(\tau^{\rm cable}_a(f) - z_a / c\big)\big]}{\sqrt{G^{\rm cable}_a(f)}} .
$$

Its components are:

- **Cable delay.** $\tau^{\rm cable}_a(f)$ is the frequency-dependent cable delay.
- **Height delay.** $z_a/c$ is the geometric delay corresponding to the height of the stand. Together with the cable delay, it phases the station to zenith.
- **Cable gain.** $G^{\rm cable}_a(f)$ is the cable attenuation model.

Two classes of stand are excluded by setting their weight to zero:

- stands with a combined status code below 33 in either polarization;
- the outlying stands 122–128, 185, 186 and 256, which keeps the aperture compact.

The F-engine samples are multiplied by $\phi_a$ during gridding.

**Flux scale.** No flux calibration is applied, and image values are in arbitrary units. The FITS keyword `BUNIT` is set to `UNCALIB`. Where a flux scale is required, Krishnan et al. (2023) normalize to the brightest A-team source, using the spectral models of Perley & Butler (2017) and the directive gain of the dipole.

**Bandpass.** No bandpass correction is applied. Pixel spectrograms consequently show a spectral gradient and slow gain variations ([Memo 013](https://github.com/epic-astronomy/Memos/blob/master/PDFs/013_searchForFRBS.pdf)). For time-domain analyses, normalization of each channel by a running median is sufficient for most purposes.

**Astrometry.** A comparison of EPIC images with images from the LWA-SV FX imager ([Memo 012](https://github.com/epic-astronomy/Memos/blob/master/PDFs/012_EPIC_Imaging_and_Localization_Comp.pdf)) found two discrepancies:

- a bulk offset of one to two pixels, which was traced to the image orientation and corrected;
- a rotation of approximately 1°–3°, which increases toward the horizon and whose origin was not identified.

Source positions at low elevation should therefore be interpreted with caution.
