title: Usage Instructions
description: How to view EPIC spectrograms of sources monitored at the Long Wavelength Array station in Sevilleta, and how to inspect CHIME/FRB events across every imaging band.
---

The EPIC imager at LWA-SV images six bands of 3.06 MHz each, starting at 16.08, 20.67, 34.45, 57.42, 71.20 and 75.80 MHz. The imager forms 128 channels per band and averages them in pairs, so the stored data have 64 channels of 47.85 kHz per band, with an image every 83.6 ms. For every source on the watch list, a 5x5 pixel grid centred on the source is extracted from each image, with all four polarisation products, and stored in a database. The spectrogram pages query that database.

## Spectrograms of a source

Open **LWA-SV** under Spectrograms and work through the three tabs.

1. **Source.** Select a source from the watch list. The Sun is monitored continuously. Other sources, such as CHIME/FRB events, are watched for a limited time. The list can be filtered to FRBs, pulsars or solar system objects, and sorted by any column. The **All days** button above the list opens a calendar. Selecting a day shows only the sources watched on that day, and the cross beside the button returns to the whole list. A dot under a day marks a CHIME/FRB event triggered on that day. The **Observation Status** column combines the watch with what EPIC recorded during it. A watched source is Active while images of it are being stored, and Not recording when it is on the watch list but no images of it have been stored in the last ten minutes, for example while the imagers are stopped. A CHIME/FRB event whose watch has ended is Completed if any band recorded data during the watch and Not observed if none did. A source added by hand that is no longer watched is Inactive.
2. **Session.** Selecting a source opens this tab with every session of its watch, newest first. Each band is a separate session, listed with the first frequency of its band. The imager runs continuously, but sessions can be split by restarts and maintenance. For sources watched continuously or added by hand, such as the Sun, the **All days** button above the list opens a calendar that narrows it to the sessions of one day. A day chosen on the Source tab is carried over.
3. **Visualization.** Selecting a session opens this tab with its first 3 minutes. Another window of up to 3 minutes within the session can be chosen and shown with **Display**. The spectrogram shows X\*X, Y\*Y or Stokes I. **Normalise channels**, on by default, shows each channel as its departure from its median over the window in units of its robust scatter, as on the Daily Digest; the images are not calibrated, so without it the channels sit at the levels of the bandpass. **Flag RFI**, on by default, masks interference automatically with the flagger of the FRB analysis, the clipping and detrending chain of the CHIME/FRB real-time search (Rafiei-Ravandi & Smith 2023), and shows the fraction of samples flagged. Flagged samples are drawn in grey. The colour range spans the 1st to 99th percentiles of the samples shown, so that a few bright samples of remaining interference do not compress the rest; the images are not flux calibrated, so no colour bar is drawn. The flagger runs in the background once per window and mode, so the page stays responsive. Because that chain standardises and detrends each channel before its thresholds, a steady carrier or a line that switches on and off looks ordinary to it, so whole channels whose typical level or variability stands more than five robust standard deviations above their neighbours are flagged first. The setting is kept while moving between windows and sessions. The camera button above the plot saves it as an image.

The address of the page records the source, session, window and tab, so returning to the page, for example from the FRB events page, reloading it or opening a shared link shows the same spectrogram.

The **UTC** and **Local** switch at the top of the page sets the time zone of every time shown and entered on the page, including the calendar days, the time inputs and the spectrogram axis. Local time is the time zone of the browser, and the choice is remembered in that browser.

The spectrogram shows the central pixel of the grid around the source. The grid is centred on the pixel nearest the source and moves by one pixel each time the source crosses a pixel boundary, every 4 to 8 minutes for most sources, so a window can contain an abrupt change in level where the grid moved. The source itself lies within half a pixel of the central pixel throughout.

### FRB sessions

For a CHIME/FRB event the Session tab shows, in place of the start and end times, a strip spanning the whole watch, with time across and the session's band from its top to its bottom edge down. The session is shaded in green and the burst is drawn as its dispersion curve through the band, in amber, bright where the session records it and faint elsewhere. Hovering over a strip gives the times. **Only sessions the burst crosses** hides the sessions that miss the burst.

For a CHIME/FRB event the Visualization tab replaces the time inputs with a timeline of the whole session. Each cell is a 3 minute window and each row is one hour, with the minutes past the hour marked above. Cells before the session starts or after it ends are drawn as dashed outlines, and a bar marks the edge of the first and last cells of the session. Only the hours in which the burst crosses the band, and one hour either side, are shown in full (the first hour when the burst misses the band during the session). The other hours are collapsed into a strip that gives their span and the long-integration Stokes I in miniature, and expands when selected or when the arrow keys step into it. Selecting a cell displays that window, and the arrow keys step through the cells. **Show all bands, dedispersed** opens the event on the FRB events page.

Cells reached by the dispersed burst in the selected band are shaded, using the DM and arrival time in the CHIME VOEvent. The line inside a shaded cell traces the burst through the band. The bar below each cell is the Stokes I of the source from the long integrations, made roughly every 3 minutes. The tab opens on the first shaded cell. When the burst never reaches the band during the session, no window is loaded until a cell is selected.

The spectrogram of an FRB session also shows the expected dispersion curve as a dashed line, which can be turned off with **Dispersion curve**.

## FRB events across all bands

**FRB events** under Spectrograms lists every CHIME/FRB event the station has watched, with the arrival time CHIME/FRB measured at 400 MHz, its DM, the number of bands analysed and the highest S/N found. The page has two tabs. **Event** lists the events; it can be sorted by any column, is split into pages, and the **All days** calendar above it, with a dot on each day that has an event, narrows it to one day. Selecting an event opens **Analysis**, the burst search in all six bands at once, and the address of the page links straight to that event. The **UTC** and **Local** switch beside the list sets the time zone of the times shown and of the calendar days.

CHIME/FRB measures the topocentric arrival time of a burst at 400 MHz, the bottom of its band, and publishes it with the event. Its arrival at infinite frequency is derived from that time and the DM. The analysis therefore uses the 400 MHz time as its reference, and computes the arrival in each EPIC band from it.

### How long an event is watched

When CHIME/FRB reports a burst, the source is added to the watch list from the burst's arrival time at infinite frequency until it has crossed the lowest imaged frequency, 16.08 MHz, plus 20%. At a DM of 1600 pc cm⁻³ the burst takes about 7 hours to reach 16 MHz, so the source is watched for about 8.5 hours. Pixel data are stored only while a source is watched, so a band the burst reaches after the watch ends has no data.

### Dedispersed products

The value of the source in each image is interpolated within its 5x5 grid at the source's exact position, which removes the abrupt changes when the grid moves by a pixel. Each channel is shifted by its dispersion delay at the CHIME DM, so a burst at that DM lines up at 0 s in every channel and band. Broadband signals that are not dispersed are removed, each channel is converted to S/N using the samples away from the expected arrival, and channels dominated by interference are blanked. The products are computed on the imaging nodes after each watch ends, with the six bands shared out between the nodes.

The page shows three things for the selected event.

- **Band summary.** The expected arrival time in each band, the dispersion smearing within one channel, the fraction of data present, the number of flagged channels and the highest S/N found near the expected arrival. Bands without data give the reason, for example when the session ended before the burst reached the band.
- **Dedispersed spectrograms.** One panel per band, highest frequency at the top. The dashed lines bound the search window, which allows for the smearing within a channel and the uncertainty of the DM. Each panel spans at least ten times the widest search filter outside the window, so the noise level is measured reliably. In the lowest bands this is up to 15 minutes either side of the burst.
- **Band-averaged time series.** The mean S/N of the unflagged channels in each band, with the highest peak in the search window marked. Smoothing helps to bring out bursts broadened by smearing or scattering.

The **Window**, **Colour range** and **Time series smoothing** controls above the plots set the time range shown, the S/N range of the colour scale and the width of the smoothing.

At low frequencies the smearing within one channel is large, about 115 s at 17 MHz for a DM of 1600 pc cm⁻³, so a burst in the lower bands appears as a broad rise rather than a sharp pulse. Peaks of S/N 3 to 5 are expected from noise alone, because each band searches many times, widths and, in the DM transform, trial DMs and pixels.

Before dedispersion, RFI is masked and the data detrended with the clipping and detrending chain of the CHIME/FRB real-time search (Rafiei-Ravandi & Smith 2023), adapted to the 64 channels and minutes-long windows of EPIC.

### Dispersion measure transform

Below the dedispersed spectrograms, the page shows a search over trial DMs around the CHIME DM, spanning ten times the DM error quoted by CHIME, but no less than 3 and no more than 25 pc cm⁻³, following the LOFAR analysis of FRB 20180916B (Pleunis et al. 2021). It covers every pixel of the 5x5 grid around the source, at the full 83.6 ms resolution.

Each band is transformed with the Fast Dispersion Measure Transform (Zackay & Ofek 2017) and its dedispersed time series are searched with boxcars up to the smearing. The bands are then combined on a common time axis, the arrival at 400 MHz relative to the time measured by CHIME/FRB, so a burst lies at 0 s at every trial DM. Because the burst takes hours to cross the six bands, the combination measures the DM far more precisely than any one band.

- **Results table.** For each band and for all bands combined, the peak S/N, the DM where it occurs and its offset from the CHIME DM, the offset of the arrival time from the CHIME arrival, the boxcar width, the fraction of data masked as RFI, the source elevation and a model fluence limit. The limit uses the radiometer equation, because the images are not flux calibrated.
- **Pixel grid.** The peak S/N of every pixel, with all bands combined. A real burst peaks on the source pixel, while RFI tends to light up the whole grid. Selecting a pixel shows its transforms.
- **S/N against trial DM.** One curve per band and one for the combination, with the CHIME DM and the S/N threshold of 7 marked.
- **DM against arrival time.** The combined transform of the selected pixel and the transform of each band for the same pixel. Time is the arrival at 400 MHz relative to CHIME/FRB, so a burst appears where the diagonal tracks of the bands cross, while a feature fixed in time within one band appears as a diagonal line.

A candidate needs S/N 7 or more in the combination, with its peak on the source pixel.

The analysis runs automatically after each watch. Each node takes the next band waiting to be analysed, and when every band of an event is done the bands are combined, usually within half an hour of the end of the watch. A summary of the result is then posted to the CHIME events channel on Slack, with a link to the event on this page.

## Daily digest

**Daily Digest** shows the spectrum of a source over a whole day, made from the long integrations the imager saves roughly every 3 minutes. It needs a source on the watch list that is above the horizon during the day selected.

The **Select a day** button opens a calendar of UTC days, in which a dot marks each day with a digest. Selecting the title of the calendar steps out from the days of a month to the months of a year and then to the years, and selecting a year or a month steps back in, so earlier dates are reached in a few steps. The calendars of the LWA-SV page work the same way.

The digest is shown as soon as a day and a source are selected, with Stokes I or V chosen from the **Stokes** list. Each band is drawn as its own panel, highest frequency at the top, on a common time axis. Every channel is expressed as its departure from its median over the day, in units of its robust scatter (1.4826 times the median absolute deviation). This removes the bandpass and the differences in sky brightness between the bands, so all panels share one colour scale, in σ, whose range is set by the 1st and 99th percentiles of all panels and limited to ±10σ. Samples where a band's output has gone dead, with the mean of |Stokes I| over its channels below 1% of its typical level, are shown as gaps and left out of the statistics. The colour map is Viridis, as in the spectrograms of the LWA-SV page. Gaps in the data are left blank.

The controls sit in the bar at the top of the page. The **UTC** and **Local** switch there sets the time zone of the time axis and of the zoom slider; the day itself is a UTC day, since each digest covers one UTC day. The slider above the panels zooms in time. Its handles set the start and end of the window, the window between them can be dragged, and **Full day** returns to the whole day. The curve behind the slider shows activity: at each time, the root mean square over every channel of every band of the normalised values shown in the panels. It is low when the spectrum is as usual and peaks at solar bursts, interference and other changes, even when they are confined to a few channels, which makes them easy to find and zoom into. By default the panels are sized to fit the window; clearing **Fit to window** gives each band a fixed height.

The **Colour exponent** slider stretches the colour map rather than the data: a value at a fraction u of the colour range is drawn with the colour at u raised to the exponent. It runs from 0.25 to 16 and starts at 4. Exponents below 1 bring out faint structure and exponents above 1 bring out the brightest. Because only the colour map changes, the slider responds immediately.
